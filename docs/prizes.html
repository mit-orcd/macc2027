The Grand Prize

$5,000

One Grand Prize, awarded across all areas, for the best use of agentic coding to advance research and education missions at MIT.

The Grand Prize goes to the entry that best answers the contest's central question - the strongest combination of contribution to its elected research or education area, technical capability and originality, and robustness and reproducibility. Every entry competing in an area is automatically in contention for the Grand Prize.

The Area Prizes

Seven Area Prizes of $2,000 each, aligned to MIT's own structure: the five schools, the Schwarzman College of Computing, and MIT Education. Each entry elects areas in which it wants to be judged. In every area, strong entries will be ones where a lab, class, or community can adopt, understand, and absorb tools that deepen human expertise and insight.

How prizes are judged

Prize finalists are judged on three things together: an open-source piece of software the team created, a short write-up, and a live presentation at the January showcase (demo and Q&A). Finalists must open-source their work to be eligible for prizes.

The three headline criteria are:

  • Contribution, or credible potential for contribution, to the elected research or education area.
  • Technical capability, innovation, and originality.
  • Robustness and reproducibility of the outcome.

Hardware prizes

On top of the seven Area Prizes and the Grand Prize, the contest will include hardware and cloud prizes. These will include DGX Spark systems, Strix Halo systems, Pro Max with GB10 systems, and more. Additional credits for cloud services will be offered as prizes.

Hardware prizes will draw from cross-cutting categories - among them best open-weight-only result, best result on a modest desktop rig, best open-source harness contribution, best verified and reproducible result, best multi-agent or swarm design, most meaningful human–machine collaboration, best example of deterministic tool creation, best learning-forward tool, and best example of robust research software engineering practices. A “noble failure” prize for the most instructive, candidly documented ambitious miss may also be awarded! Details will be announced here.
